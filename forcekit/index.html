Only in ForceKIT

What you won't find in other Salesforce extensions.

The everyday tools we couldn't find anywhere else — so we built them right into the pages you already use.

01

A command palette on every Salesforce page

Press Ctrl+K anywhere in Salesforce: jump to Setup pages, apps, records and saved links, run your scripts, open your notes. Search records as you type and keep your favourite destinations in colored groups.

  • Shortcuts in groups, the same in all your orgs
  • Record search with instant results
  • Apps missing from an org are flagged, not broken
02

Notes that keep your work together

Write notes right on the page and attach what you're working on — saved queries, Apex scripts, debug logs, org access and screenshots — each in its place in the text. Checklists, priorities, groups and reminders included.

  • Capture an area of the page straight into the note
  • Export to PDF or Word
  • Share by link, optionally password-protected — no server involved
03

Apex scripts with a real input form

Turn anonymous Apex into a tool for the whole team: {{fields}} become text boxes, choices and checkboxes, a script can be split into ordered steps or independent actions, and System.debug values show up as outputs you copy with one click. Run them on the record you're looking at, right inside the page.

  • Steps in order, or actions to use when you need them
  • Find & replace, formatting and syntax colors in the editor
  • {{$SessionId}} for callouts to the same org, never saved in the script
04

Bubbles that follow you around

Drag a script, note or shortcut out of the palette — or middle-click it — and it becomes a floating bubble that stays while you move between pages. Bubbles snap together into a column, fan out on hover, and go away together when you drop them on the trash.

  • Scripts, notes and shortcuts
  • They stay across page changes in the same tab
  • Double-click a note bubble to reopen it in the palette
05

Record what you do on a page

Start Record actions, use the page as usual, and ForceKIT turns your clicks and inputs into a script you can replay, edit and keep — handy for repetitive data entry and test setups.

  • From the side panel, or as a palette shortcut
  • Review the recorded steps before saving
  • Runs again on the page, like any other script

06

Debug logs, even by the thousand

Filter logs by date, size and user, get true counts even with thousands of logs, delete everything that matches a filter with progress and stop, attach a log to a note, and open any script's full log in the viewer with search and the execution tree.

  • Date and size filters side by side
  • Full storage? ForceKIT offers to free space and retry
  • Log levels and users under debug in one place

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about ForceKIT and your Salesforce orgs.

What is ForceKIT?

ForceKIT is a free Chrome extension for Salesforce (SFDC) admins and developers. It adds a command palette to every Salesforce page and brings a SOQL console, debug log tools, anonymous Apex scripts, notes and a profile comparator together in one side panel.

What is the ForceKIT Marketplace?

A public catalog of official Apex and JavaScript scripts. You can read every script's code, and ForceKIT checks each file's SHA-256 signature before installing it in Scripts with one click; when a new version comes out, you'll see the update.

How do I run SOQL queries in Chrome?

Open the ForceKIT side panel and use the SOQL console: it autocompletes objects and fields, keeps your queries in tabs and history, and lets you edit, export or copy the results. It uses the session of the org you're already logged into — no extra login.

How can I view and filter Salesforce debug logs?

The Debug Logs tab lists your org's logs with filters by user, date and size — even past 2,000 records — and opens each log in a full viewer. You can delete logs in bulk and jump from a script run straight to its log.

Can I run anonymous Apex from the browser?

Yes. Save your Apex scripts in ForceKIT and run them from the side panel, the command palette or a floating bubble on any Salesforce page. Scripts can ask for values with input forms and picklists, and can be split into steps or independent actions.

Is ForceKIT an alternative to Salesforce Inspector?

It covers the same everyday needs — SOQL queries, record details with API names, data import, debug logs — and adds a command palette, notes with attachments, Apex scripts with input forms, floating bubbles and an action recorder. You can use it on its own or alongside other Salesforce extensions.

Does it work with sandboxes and multiple orgs?

Yes. ForceKIT works in production and sandbox orgs and recognizes the org of the current tab. Save the orgs you use most to switch between them quickly, pin your favourites and use Login As on users in one click.

Is my Salesforce data safe?

ForceKIT has no server of its own: requests go straight from your browser to your org, and what it saves stays on your device. There are no analytics and no tracking; optional sync uses your own Chrome Sync or Google Drive.

Is ForceKIT free?

Yes, completely: no premium tier, no account and no ads. Install it from the Chrome Web Store — and if it saves you time, you can support the project with a coffee.

No server, no accounts, no tracking

Your org, your browser, your data.

ForceKIT has no server of its own. The only network traffic it makes goes directly from your browser to your own Salesforce org, or to Google's Chrome Sync or your own Google Drive if you turn one of those on yourself. Nothing is sent to us, because there is no "us" to send it to.

Shared note links carry the note inside the link itself, after the #, which browsers never send to any server.
